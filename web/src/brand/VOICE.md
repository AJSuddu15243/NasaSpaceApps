# Lunala · Voice

Calm, precise and useful to someone making a comparison. Explain the observation first,
then the implication. Use the name **Lunala** and the descriptor **Lunar Landing
Site Navigator** on first introduction. Tagline: **Compare sites. Find your window.**

These are copy patterns, not measured results. Replace braces only with verified values;
keep mock or unavailable results visibly labelled.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with the observation and its window: “Sunlit for {percent}% of the {hours} h window.” | Call a site “perfect”, “safe” or “optimal” without stating the criteria. |
| Put units and a reference beside a number: “Solar elevation: {angle}° above the local horizontal.” | Show a bare number or imply an apparent terrain horizon is the local horizontal. |
| Give a concrete next action: “Choose a site”, “Compare dates”, “Retry terrain download”. | Use vague calls to action such as “Unlock possibilities” or “Explore more”. |
| State a model limit where it affects the answer: “Simplified temperature estimate; no thermal inertia.” | Call an estimate a measurement or imply NASA has certified the result. |
| Distinguish missing data from a result: “No rock data for this layer; rock weight is zero.” | Treat missing data as zero hazard, hide failed requests, or rely on status colour alone. |

In Explorer mode, explain “direct-to-Earth” as “a clear view of Earth for communication”.
In Planner mode, retain the term and show the assumption that a DSN station can see the
Moon. Use UTC for times and name it. Prefer “meets these thresholds” to “safe to land”.
